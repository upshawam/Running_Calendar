# NYC Marathon 2026 — Sub-4:00 Training Check-In

## Goal

**Race:** NYC Marathon — November 1, 2026  
**Primary goal:** Sub-4:00  
**Training marathon pace:** ~9:05–9:10/mi  
**Desired buffer:** Fitness around 3:55–3:58

This checklist is designed to determine whether the training is translating into realistic sub-4 marathon readiness. The key tests are not simply about hitting mileage; they evaluate pace control, durability, fatigue resistance, and recovery.

---

## Weekly health/recovery check

For every week, record:

- [ ] Completed planned running without unusual pain
- [ ] Normal soreness resolved within ~24–48 hours
- [ ] Easy pace/effort remained genuinely easy
- [ ] No progressive tendon/joint pain
- [ ] Could complete the next quality session without carrying excessive fatigue
- [ ] Sleep/recovery remained reasonably normal
- [ ] Long-run fueling was practiced successfully

### Rule of thumb

A workout that looks great on paper but leaves you unable to train normally afterward is **not** a successful workout.

# Goal Decision

### 🟢 GREEN — Go for sub-4

If most major checkpoints are GREEN and especially if:

- Checkpoint 4 is GREEN/VERY GREEN
- Checkpoint 5 is GREEN/VERY GREEN
- Checkpoint 6 is GREEN

**Race plan:** Start conservatively and target approximately 9:05–9:15/mi through the middle of the race.

---

### 🟡 YELLOW — Sub-4 is possible; race conservatively

If the long runs are strong but MP remains somewhat harder than expected:

**Race plan:** Start around 9:10–9:15 and reassess after mile 16–18.

Do not force 9:05 simply because the watch says you need it.

---

### 🔴 RED — Don't force the clock

If Checkpoint 5 breaks down substantially or MP still feels like threshold effort late in the cycle:

**Priority:** Enjoy NYC and run by effort.

A 4:05–4:15 marathon with your wife is preferable to turning the race into a prolonged sufferfest chasing a goal the training did not support.

# Race-Day Mental Check

Before the race:

- [ ] I do not need to prove anything in the first 5 miles.
- [ ] 9:20–9:30 early is acceptable.
- [ ] The marathon begins after the halfway point.
- [ ] Hills should be run by effort, not forced pace.
- [ ] I will fuel before I feel like I need fuel.
- [ ] I have enough fitness to be patient.
- [ ] The goal is to enjoy running NYC with my wife while giving sub-4 a legitimate attempt.

## Most important signal

**The goal is not to see whether you can run 20 miles. You already have 50-mile experience.**

The most important question is:

> **Can you run approximately 9:05–9:10/mi after substantial accumulated training and still feel like you have another gear?**

That is the signal to pay the closest attention to.
